Find papers

Search Keywords

How keywords work

An LLM reads your research interest + titles and abstracts of all papers in My Collection (up to {{ reference_papers_limit }}, priority-ordered by tag), then outputs 3–6 short search phrases (for the arXiv search, and the bioRxiv search if it is switched on). The more papers you have tagged, the better the vocabulary.

Keywords are saved and reused by the nightly crawl automatically. Regenerate only when your research focus changes significantly.

Paper Crawler

How crawling works

  1. arXiv keyword search: Queries arXiv once for all keyword phrases from the Keywords widget above (a paper matching any of them counts), restricted to your date range; deduplicates results by ID. arXiv returns at most 200 results per query, so a search that hits that cap continues further back in the date range. Every request to arXiv (search, HTML, PDF) goes through one global lock with at least 5 s between requests. If another crawl or search is active you'll see "Waiting for arXiv…" below. If arXiv is rate limiting or busy, the search waits and tries again (after 2, then 4 minutes); if it still gets no answer, that is reported as a warning, and the nightly crawl tries again twice more at the end of the run.
  2. bioRxiv keyword search (off unless ticked; the option under Nightly crawl sets it for the nightly run and for Find Papers): bioRxiv cannot search by keyword, so the whole date range is read from its public API and a paper is kept when its title or abstract contains one of your keywords as a phrase, as in the arXiv search (a plural "s" and hyphens are ignored). Only first versions count. Its full text is read from bioRxiv like an arXiv paper's: the HTML version, or the PDF while there is none yet (bioRxiv adds the HTML a few days after posting); one download every 7 s, as bioRxiv asks. Figure 1 comes from the HTML version too, so for a fresh preprint it shows up a few days later (the nightly run looks for it until 30 days after posting). Its card links to bioRxiv.
  3. Semantic Scholar recommendations (if you have papers in My Collection): fetches papers similar to your collection via the Semantic Scholar recommendations API, from up to 100 My Collection papers in groups of 10 (the API ranks by similarity, not date, so smaller groups reach more recent papers). A My Collection paper that is not on arXiv is used by its DOI (or found on Semantic Scholar by its title). The recommendations in your date range are kept: arXiv papers, bioRxiv preprints (full text read as for the bioRxiv search) and journal papers with a DOI and an abstract (judged on the abstract, unless their PDF is openly available from a site on the open-access list).
  4. Papers citing My Collection (the "Citations" source): for each paper in My Collection that Semantic Scholar knows (up to 50 per crawl, the ones checked longest ago first), asks Semantic Scholar which papers cite it, and keeps the ones dated in your date range, the same kinds as for the recommendations. One request per My Collection paper (the groups of 10 are only for the recommendations), so this is the slowest source. A paper found by several sources is screened once; its card says how it was found ("Found via keyword …", "Semantic Scholar recommendation", "cites …").
  5. Quick relevance filter: LLM receives your research interest + My Collection paper titles/abstracts + the candidate paper's title and abstract. Returns {"relevant": true/false}. 
    {{ prompt_relevance_quick }}
  6. Deep relevance check (only papers that pass the quick filter, so full texts are only fetched for those): same prompt plus the full paper content (arXiv and bioRxiv: the HTML version, else the PDF; a journal paper: its PDF if it is openly available from a site on the open-access list, else the abstract again, and its card says "⚠ from abstract only"). For a relevant paper it also says why it matters for your research interest; that is shown on the card as "Why it's relevant". 
    {{ prompt_relevance_deep }}
  7. Screening cache: Papers already in this project (New Papers, My Collection or Trash) are never added again. Before calling the LLM, each crawl also computes a short hash of your research interest + the titles of your My Collection papers; papers already rejected under the same hash are skipped without an LLM call. The hash changes (and rejected papers get a new chance) when you edit the research interest or add/remove a paper in My Collection. To re-check papers already in New Papers after such a change, use Re-screen there.
  8. Summary: For papers that pass both checks, the LLM writes a neutral 3-sentence summary plus the badges (dataset, method, …), and in a second call the main contributions. A paper's summary is shared by every project it is in, so it does not use your research interest; the per-project "Why it's relevant" line does. 
    {{ prompt_summary }}
  9. Institutions: For arXiv papers, a single batch call to the Semantic Scholar graph API fetches author affiliations for all newly added papers. Falls back to LLM extraction from page 1 of the PDF (which contains author/affiliation headers and footnotes) if Semantic Scholar returns nothing. A bioRxiv paper gets its corresponding author's institution from bioRxiv; a journal paper gets none.
  10. Featured paper: LLM picks the single most relevant paper from all newly added ones — considering your research interest, and preferring papers with a figure — to highlight in the crawl-complete email.
  11. Email: If any new papers were added, a crawl-complete notification is sent with stats and the featured paper (one email per project per night, with a warning if some keywords couldn't be searched). You can also email any single paper with its Notify button.

How tagging improves future crawls

Papers in My Collection (up to {{ reference_papers_limit }}, Important first, then Discuss, then To Read) are used as reference context in the relevance check prompts. The more you curate, the more precisely the LLM understands your research scope. They also seed the Semantic Scholar recommendations and citations (by arXiv id, or by DOI for a paper that is not on arXiv) — more tagged papers means better discovery of related work.

What happens when you paste a URL

  1. arXiv URL — fetched directly from arXiv. Full enrichment: figure, citation count, page count, LLM summary & contributions, author institutions.
  2. bioRxiv URL — becomes the same paper a bioRxiv crawl would store: DOI, institution and posting date from bioRxiv, full text (HTML version, else PDF) for the summary, figure 1 (added later by the nightly run if bioRxiv has no HTML version yet), citation count.
  3. Any other URL (PDF, ACL, NeurIPS, blog…)
    1. HTML page is fetched. If it has an arXiv link that matches the title → treated as arXiv paper (full enrichment above).
    2. If no arXiv link but a DOI is present (citation_doi meta tag) → Semantic Scholar is queried. If Scholar knows an arXiv preprint for that DOI → full arXiv enrichment.
    3. Otherwise arXiv is searched for the page's title; a matching title → full arXiv enrichment.
    4. If no arXiv match → stored as a web paper. Title, authors, abstract (from citation_abstract only — not site taglines), and the stripped page body are extracted. LLM generates a summary from the full page text. No figure, no citation count. The PDF (for page count and institutions) is only downloaded from open-access hosts (arXiv, bioRxiv, ACL Anthology, PMLR, OpenReview, NeurIPS, CVF, AAAI, IJCAI, JMLR). Its DOI (or, without one, a Semantic Scholar title match) makes it a seed for the Semantic Scholar recommendations and citations, like an arXiv paper.
    5. If the publisher blocks the page (ScienceDirect, Cell, Wiley and others answer automated requests with an error page) or it has no abstract → title, abstract and authors come from OpenAlex and Crossref via the paper's DOI (for Elsevier links, the DOI is found from the article id in the link). No publisher page or PDF is fetched for this; the summary is then made from the abstract.

All papers are tagged Important on add and skip LLM screening.

How research interest generation works

If you already have text in the box, Generate refines your existing description into at most about 300 words (a longer text gets condensed), using papers in My Collection as silent background context — the output will not mention specific papers or authors, only ideas, problems, methods, and goals.

If the box is empty, the LLM reads titles and abstracts of all papers in My Collection (up to {{ reference_papers_limit }}, priority-ordered by tag) and writes a research interest from scratch (at most about 300 words).

The research interest goes into every screening prompt, so it is limited to {{ "{:,}".format(research_interest_max) }} characters.

Only My Collection is used: New Papers and Trash are ignored.

Tip: the more diverse and well-tagged your collection, the more precise the generated description.
